Rulebook

How Kehai Care decides

Rules as of 30 September 2026 · 36 rules · 53 symptoms

If this is an emergency, call 911. This page explains how the app reaches an answer. It is not a way to check your own symptoms, and it is not medical advice.

How the rules are used

You describe how you feel. An on-device model picks out symptoms from a fixed list of 53, only ones grounded in words you actually said, and shows them to you to confirm or correct. Then the rules below run. They are ordinary code written down in advance, so the same confirmed symptoms always produce the same answer.

  • Every rule whose conditions you meet applies, and the most urgent one wins. Nothing you add can lower it.
  • Apple Health readings can raise the level by one step, never lower it.
  • Answers to follow-up questions are recorded for you to pass on. Only a measured temperature changes a level.
  • If nothing below applies, the answer is care at home, with what to watch for and a reminder to check again.
  • Crisis language goes straight to 988 and is never saved.

Review status

22 of 36 rules were reviewed by a physician assistant in August 2026. The rest were built from published guidelines and have not yet been reviewed by a clinician. Each rule below says which applies to it. The app says the same thing in its onboarding and on every result, under “What led to this.”

Call 911

RF001 Chest pain with associated cardiac symptom

Applies when you report pain pressure tightness or squeezing in the chest; and any one of: pain spreading to arm shoulder neck or jaw; sudden cold sweat not explained by exertion or heat; shortness of breath or difficulty breathing; feeling sick to the stomach with or without vomiting.

Source: Emergency Severity Index level 2 · DP-B high risk acute chest pain concerning for ischemia

Reviewed by a physician assistant, August 2026. A cardiac nurse also gave feedback on this rule.

RF032 One-sided leg swelling with breathlessness

Applies when you report new swelling, tenderness or warmth in one leg but not the other; and shortness of breath or difficulty breathing.

Source: Emergency Severity Index level 2 · Guideline - CDC venous thromboembolism literature. Unilateral leg swelling with new breathlessness suggests DVT that has embolized to PE, which carries sudden obstructive shock and cardiac arrest risk. Encoding unreviewed by a clinician.

Not yet reviewed by a clinician. Built from the published guideline named in its source.

RF033 Pain out of proportion to appearance

Applies when you report pain far worse than the area looks, or redness spreading quickly.

Source: Emergency Severity Index level 2 · Guideline - IDSA Practice Guidelines for Skin and Soft Tissue Infections / LRINEC scoring system. Pain far exceeding visible exam findings is the classic sentinel for necrotizing fasciitis and other necrotizing soft tissue infections, which carry reported mortality around 15-40% and require immediate surgical debridement, not same-day outpatient evaluation. Escalated from ED-today to call-911 on independent verification against IDSA source material. Encoding unreviewed by a clinician.

Not yet reviewed by a clinician. Built from the published guideline named in its source.

RF034 Cardiac symptoms without classic chest pain

Applies when you report burning in the chest or throat, often after eating; and sudden cold sweat not explained by exertion or heat.

Source: Emergency Severity Index level 2 · Guideline - ACC/AHA Joint Guideline for the Evaluation and Diagnosis of Chest Pain (2021/2025). Burning epigastric discomfort with diaphoresis is a common presentation of myocardial ischemia in women, older adults, and people with diabetes. Label changed from 'atypical' because the guideline explicitly recommends against that term, since it has been shown to make patients and clinicians underestimate severity. Mirrors RF001's logic applied to the epigastric variant. Encoding unreviewed by a clinician.

Not yet reviewed by a clinician. Built from the published guideline named in its source. A cardiac nurse gave feedback on an earlier version.

RF035 Flank pain with fever or instability

Applies when you report pain in the side or back below the ribs; and active vomiting; and any one of: elevated body temperature; fainting or blacking out, any duration; new confusion disorientation or lethargy versus baseline.

Source: Emergency Severity Index level 2 · Guideline - ACEP renal colic literature. Fever or an unstable finding alongside flank pain and vomiting suggests infection behind an obstruction, which can progress to sepsis quickly. Encoding unreviewed by a clinician.

Not yet reviewed by a clinician. Built from the published guideline named in its source.

RF036 Vaginal bleeding with pain or instability in possible pregnancy

Only when pregnancy is possible.

Source: Emergency Severity Index level 2 · Guideline - ACOG ectopic pregnancy criteria. Bleeding combined with pain or an instability sign is the specific pattern associated with rupture. Encoding unreviewed by a clinician.

Raise the level by one

RF017 Danger zone vital signs with any symptom

With any symptom, if Apple Health shows heart rate over 100, breathing rate over 20 per minute, or blood oxygen under 92%. Readings can only raise the level, never lower it, and readings taken during exercise are set aside.

Only when Apple Health readings are available.

Source: Emergency Severity Index level 2 · DP-D danger zone vital signs upgrade consideration

Reviewed by a physician assistant, August 2026.

Heard, but never the only reason

These are recorded and shown back to you, but none of them can decide a level on its own:

  • Aching or pain in the back
  • Throat is sore or scratchy
  • Coughing, dry or with mucus
  • Head pain that is not sudden or worst-ever
  • Worn out or low on energy despite rest
  • Joints stiff, swollen or achy
  • Stuffy or runny nose, sinus pressure
  • Ear pain, pressure or fullness
  • Rash, hives or irritated skin
  • Sore or aching muscles
  • Burning in the chest or throat, often after eating
  • Ringing, buzzing or humming in the ears
  • Not wanting to eat, or feeling full very quickly
